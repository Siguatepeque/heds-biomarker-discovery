Headline result: using only papers published through 2021 - roughly 3.5 years before
the first hEDS GWAS meta-analysis appeared on medRxiv (19 Sept 2025) - the pipeline
flags SLC39A13 as a candidate gene for hEDS/HSD via a purely indirect literature path.

This result went through two rounds of correction before I trusted it. Both are worth
recording, because they're the actual substance of the validation work:

ROUND 1 - found via an unrelated exploratory run: loosening the co-occurrence threshold
surfaced KLK15 (another 2024 hEDS gene) as a weak signal. Tracing it back led to PMID
40949095 (iScience, Aug 2025), titled "KLK15 alters connective tissues in hypermobile
Ehlers-Danlos syndrome" - i.e. a paper that directly, explicitly studies KLK15 in hEDS.
PubTator tags the gene correctly but normalizes the disease mention "hypermobile
Ehlers-Danlos syndrome" down to the generic Ehlers-Danlos syndrome MeSH ID (shared by
every subtype) rather than a hEDS-specific one. The pipeline's seed detection was
matching against the graph's single stored display name per entity ID - which is
whichever surface text happened to be recorded first, an arbitrary and unreliable
choice for a high-degree ID with many different real-world phrasings. Fix: seed
detection now scans each document's own raw annotation text directly
(discover_candidates.seed_documents), not the graph's aggregated per-ID name.

ROUND 2 - re-running the SLC39A13 backtest after that fix initially showed it as
"already directly studied" by 2022, which would have meant no prediction case at all.
Investigating why turned up two problems in my own SEED_PATTERNS list:
  1. The pattern "ehlers-danlos syndrome type 3" (meant to catch the old pre-2017 name
     for hEDS) is a naive substring match, so it also matched "Ehlers-Danlos syndrome,
     spondylodysplastic form type 3" - a different, specific EDS subtype that SLC39A13
     is actually already known to cause. Fix: added "spondylodysplastic" /
     "spondylo-dysplastic" to EXCLUDE_PATTERNS.
  2. "generalized joint hypermobility" (GJH) was in SEED_PATTERNS, but GJH is a
     clinical sign, not a diagnosis - PMID 31594391 explicitly studies GJH as a
     population distinct from EDS ("crucial to distinguish the genetic basis of GJH
     from... EDS"). Fix: removed it from SEED_PATTERNS (kept in fetch_literature.QUERY,
     which only controls corpus scope, not seed status - a paper can legitimately
     belong in the corpus without counting as direct evidence of studying hEDS itself).

After both fixes, the result held up under direct verification: no document before 2022
ever co-mentions an hEDS/HSD seed term and SLC39A13 in the same abstract (confirmed by
querying the graph directly), and neither of the two source papers that discuss
SLC39A13 (PMID 32295219, Genes (Basel) 2020; PMID 31594391, Connect Tissue Res 2021) is
itself a seed document. The connection is built from several independently weak but
real bridge concepts shared between actual hEDS/HSD seed documents and these two
papers - most substantially "juvenile connective tissue diseases" (MeSH D003240, a
legitimate bridge appearing in 54 hEDS/HSD seed documents, well under the hub-degree
cutoff, with a weight-2 edge to SLC39A13), plus weaker single-mention contributions via
tenascin-X, COL1A1, and "type I procollagen." The Adamic-Adar score (0.86) is the sum
of 1/log(degree) over all of these, and was hand-verified against the raw graph to
confirm the arithmetic, not just the code path.

Independent confirmation: Petrucci-Nelson et al., "Complex Genetics and Regulatory
Drivers of Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome," medRxiv 2025.09.19.25336146, found
genome-wide-significant signal at SLC39A13 in a ~1,800-case/~5,000-control GWAS
meta-analysis - published nearly four years after the literature trail this pipeline
follows was already in place, using a completely different method (genotyping, not
literature mining).

Honest limitation: SLC39A13 was not a total unknown going in - it's the established
cause of a different, rare, recessive EDS subtype (spondylocheirodysplastic EDS), which
is exactly why it had enough of an indirect literature trail for this method to reach
it. ACKR3, the GWAS's other major locus, has no connective-tissue literature footprint
before 2025 at all (still absent even at the 2026 cutoff) and this method correctly has
nothing to say about it. KLK15 has exactly one paper with a matched disease mention in
the entire corpus (the Aug 2025 iScience paper itself) - one paper is one paper, not
enough independent evidence to clear the 2-document noise floor, and correctly doesn't.
Literature-based discovery can get ahead of the field when a candidate has *some*
indirect textual trail to follow, however thin; it has nothing to offer for a
genuinely de novo finding with no prior trace, and this method is honest about the
difference rather than forcing a result either way.

CURRENT STATUS CHECK (run against the full corpus, no cutoff): SLC39A13 is still
classified as a candidate today, not yet "already studied," even eleven months after
the GWAS. Exactly one document in the entire corpus directly co-mentions an hEDS/HSD
seed term and SLC39A13 in the same abstract - PMID 41001447, the GWAS preprint itself
(medRxiv, 21 Sept 2025). The 2-document noise floor this pipeline applies to "already
studied" hasn't been cleared yet, because no second paper has echoed the connection
directly. That's a real, separate finding worth sitting with: the literature index can
lag a genuine discovery by a year or more even after publication, which is exactly the
gap a tool like this is positioned to notice.
