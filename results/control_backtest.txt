Headline result: across all 8 cutoffs, including the full present-day corpus, the
pipeline never flags a single one of fibromyalgia's 8 real GWAS-implicated genes as a
candidate. Zero false "years-early" flags on the disease this control was built to
stress-test.

That is the result this control was designed to produce. It is a clean negative, not
an artifact of an empty corpus: by 2026 the corpus has 4,245 documents and 45,373 edges
(bigger than the entire hEDS corpus), all 8 target genes are present in the graph, and
the method still declines to call any of them a candidate. The one gene that changes
status, DCC, moves straight from "present but below the bridge floor" to "already
directly studied" - i.e., some 2026 document (almost certainly the GWAS paper itself,
the same pattern SLC39A13 showed in the real hEDS backtest) co-mentions DCC and
fibromyalgia directly, which files it as already-studied rather than surfacing it as a
novel indirect candidate. The other 7 stay "present but not a candidate" through 2026:
they're in the literature, connected to fibromyalgia's discourse, but only through
generic high-degree bridge terms (degree 16 by 2026) that the Adamic-Adar weighting is
specifically built to discount.

Established-gene source: Kerrebijn et al., "The genetic architecture of fibromyalgia
across 2.5 million individuals" (2,563,755 individuals, 54,629 cases, 11 cohorts) -
preprinted on medRxiv 2025-09-19 (doi:10.1101/2025.09.18.25335914), published in Nature
Medicine 2026-07-28 (doi:10.1038/s41591-026-04492-6). One study, not two (an earlier
draft of this file cited a second "Nat Commun 2026" paper for the same finding; that
was wrong and has been corrected - see control_backtest.py's comment history). The
strongest hit was a coding variant in HTT (the Huntington's disease gene); GPR52, DCC,
DRD2, NCAM1, MDGA2, and CELF4 were also prioritized. Before this paper, the GWAS
Catalog had zero fibromyalgia associations, which is why this disease was chosen as the
control in the first place (see controls.py) - the "no known genetic basis" framing was
true when this project's design was decided, and stayed true until five months before
this backtest was actually run.

What about the 6 candidates the live pipeline DID surface (results/control_candidates.csv)?
Nfe2l2 (Nrf2), Bax, S100A7, PPARGC1A (PGC-1alpha), Myog, IL23A - none STRING-connected
to a hEDS/EDS-family gene (not applicable here, that check is hEDS-specific and
uninformative for this disease), none with a ClinVar connective-tissue record, and none
of them match any of the 8 Kerrebijn et al. GWAS genes. I also checked them against the
fibromyalgia genetics literature that predates the 2025-26 GWAS - the largest prior
candidate-gene association study (PMID 21905019) and the al-Ándalus susceptibility
project both implicate a different gene set entirely (GCH1, COMT, OPRM1 among others).
None of the 6 pipeline candidates overlap with that list either. What they have in
common instead is thematic: Nrf2, Bax, and PGC-1alpha are recurring nodes in the
"oxidative stress / mitochondrial dysfunction in fibromyalgia" mechanistic-hypothesis
literature, a popular but non-genetic framing that runs through hundreds of
fibromyalgia abstracts. That is very plausibly why they cleared the bridge-degree
filter: not because they're a real bridge to a hidden causal gene, but because
"oxidative stress" and "apoptosis" function as this corpus's version of a generic hub
concept, similar in kind (though evidently not filtered as aggressively as raw hub
terms like "pain") to what discover_candidates.py's max-bridge-degree cutoff is meant
to catch.

What this does and does not prove:

- It does NOT prove the method is correct on hEDS. A negative control on a different
  disease cannot validate a positive result on hEDS; that's what the SLC39A13 backtest
  in results/backtest.txt is for. This control answers a narrower, different question:
  does the pipeline manufacture "years-early" candidates for any disease pointed at it,
  or does it stay quiet when a disease's real genetic architecture isn't there yet to
  find? For fibromyalgia, it stayed quiet - it did not backfill the 8 real GWAS genes
  years before they were confirmed, the way it did for SLC39A13 in hEDS.
- It does NOT prove the 6 fibromyalgia candidates are meaningless. It's plausible one
  of them is a genuine, still-unconfirmed lead - that's exactly the kind of claim this
  method can't distinguish from noise without a future GWAS to check against, same as
  VWF and C1R for hEDS. What the control does establish is that they aren't a repeat of
  a genetic signal that already exists and was simply missed.
- It DOES show a real limitation worth naming: the method has no defense against a
  large, thematically coherent, non-genetic literature (here, oxidative-stress
  mechanism papers) generating candidates that look structurally identical to a real
  bridge. Bridge degree alone doesn't distinguish "specific but wrong" from "specific
  and right." That's a fair thing to hold the hEDS candidate list to a higher standard
  on, not just take on faith because the SLC39A13 backtest worked once.
