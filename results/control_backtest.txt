What the tables above record: across all 8 cutoffs, the pipeline never filed one
of fibromyalgia's 8 GWAS-implicated genes as a candidate. That outcome is a
coverage and recall limit, not a specificity result. It does not show the method
avoids false positives in general, and it does not validate the hEDS result. The
early cutoffs test almost nothing: before 2019 and before 2020 the corpus holds 1
document each. By 2026 the corpus reaches 4,245 documents and 45,373 edges, all 8
target genes are present in the graph, and the method still files none of them as
a candidate. DCC changes to the old "already directly studied" label at the
2026 cutoff. This report does not identify the papers responsible. The other
seven remain present but not candidates. The saved degree counts alone do not
explain whether missing repeated edges or excluded bridge nodes caused the misses.

Established-gene source: Kerrebijn et al., "The genetic architecture of fibromyalgia
across 2.5 million individuals" (2,563,755 individuals, 54,629 cases, 11 cohorts) -
preprinted on medRxiv 2025-09-19 (doi:10.1101/2025.09.18.25335914), published in Nature
Medicine 2026-07-28 (doi:10.1038/s41591-026-04492-6). One study, not two (an earlier
draft of this file cited a second "Nat Commun 2026" paper for the same finding; that
was wrong and has been corrected - see control_backtest.py's comment history). The
strongest hit was a coding variant in HTT (the Huntington's disease gene); GPR52, DCC,
DRD2, NCAM1, MDGA2, and CELF4 were also prioritized. The original absence-of-known-genes
rationale cannot turn these GWAS targets into negative controls. Retrieving them
would count as a hit under the same evaluation used for hEDS.

What about the 6 candidates the archived live pipeline DID surface (results/control_candidates.csv)?
Nfe2l2 (Nrf2), Bax, S100A7, PPARGC1A (PGC-1alpha), Myog, IL23A - none STRING-connected
to a hEDS/EDS-family gene (not applicable here, that check is hEDS-specific and
uninformative for this disease), none with a ClinVar connective-tissue record, and none
of them match any of the 8 Kerrebijn et al. GWAS genes. They were also checked against the
fibromyalgia genetics literature that predates the 2025-26 GWAS - the largest prior
candidate-gene association study (PMID 21905019) and the al-Andalus susceptibility
project both implicate a different gene set entirely (GCH1, COMT, OPRM1 among others).
None of the 6 pipeline candidates overlap with that list either. What they have in
common instead is thematic: Nrf2, Bax, and PGC-1alpha recur in the
"oxidative stress / mitochondrial dysfunction in fibromyalgia" mechanistic-hypothesis
literature, a popular but non-genetic framing that runs through hundreds of
fibromyalgia abstracts. Those themes plausibly cleared the bridge-degree filter, not
as a bridge to a hidden causal gene but as this corpus's version of a generic hub
concept, adjacent to what discover_candidates.py's max-bridge-degree cutoff is meant
to catch.

What this does and does not show:

- It does not validate the method on hEDS. A control on a different disease cannot
  confirm a result on hEDS; the SLC39A13 backtest in results/backtest.txt is the
  separate check for that, with the limits stated there.
- It does not show the 6 fibromyalgia candidates are meaningless. One of them could
  be an unconfirmed lead. This method cannot separate that case from noise without
  independent evidence to check against. Non-overlap with the selected GWAS genes
  does not establish novelty or exclude a role in protein-level disease processes.
- It does show a limitation: the method has no defense against a large, thematically
  coherent, non-genetic literature generating candidates that look structurally
  identical to a genetic bridge. Bridge degree alone does not separate a specific
  but wrong bridge from a specific and right one. That limitation applies to the
  hEDS candidate list as well.
